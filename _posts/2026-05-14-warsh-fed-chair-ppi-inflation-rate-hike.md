## The Numbers Don't Lie

On the same day the Senate confirmed Kevin Warsh as Federal Reserve Chair in a 54-45 vote — the closest in the modern era — April's Producer Price Index landed like a grenade.

PPI: **+1.4% month-over-month**. Consensus was +0.5%. The April CPI, released the prior day, came in at **3.8% year-over-year**, also above forecast. Together they represent the sharpest back-to-back inflation data prints since the 2022 rate-hike cycle.

S&P 500 futures dropped from 7,430 to 7,392 immediately post-release before recovering to 7,418 at the cash open. The Nasdaq held green, lifted by Nvidia. But the rate market told the real story: probability of a Fed hike by December 2026 jumped to **39%**, while rate cut expectations for the year were effectively erased.

This is not a narrative about politics. It is a data event with a clear market implication.

## What Changed Under the Surface

![Inflation & Rate Hike Key Metrics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jeonghun-project/global-tech-blog/main/assets/images/stats_ppi_rate_hike.png)

The April PPI surge is structurally significant for three reasons.

**First, PPI leads CPI.** When producers pay more for energy and services, those costs eventually pass through to consumers. The April PPI spike was broad-based — not just energy but services inflation as well, which is stickier and harder to reverse. This suggests May and June CPI prints will remain elevated.

**Second, the energy component is geopolitically driven.** The Iran conflict has pushed energy costs to multi-year highs. This is not a demand-side inflation problem that the Fed can easily address with rate hikes — but the Fed has only one tool, and the market is now betting it uses it.

**Third, this follows the trend.** April CPI at 3.8% YoY was above the 3.5% consensus. Two consecutive months of above-consensus CPI and PPI signal that the disinflation trend of 2025 has stalled and potentially reversed. The Fed's 2% target is now further away than it was six months ago.

## Warsh: Hawk at the Helm

![Fed Chair Transition: Powell vs Warsh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jeonghun-project/global-tech-blog/main/assets/images/comparison_powell_warsh.png)

Warsh served on the Fed Board of Governors from 2006 to 2011. His voting record during the 2008-2010 financial crisis was unambiguously hawkish — he favored rate increases even as unemployment surged, citing inflation risks. This is not revisionist history; it is the documented record.

His declared agenda for what he calls "regime change" at the Fed includes:

- **Ending regular press conferences** after FOMC meetings — eliminating the forward guidance channel that markets have relied on for a decade
- **Abandoning PCE as the primary inflation gauge**, which he dismissed as "a rough swag as to what was going on"
- **Eliminating forward guidance** — no more "dot plots" or rate path signals

The market implication: **central bank uncertainty premium is now higher**. Investors can no longer rely on Fed communication to front-run policy. Volatility in rates — and by extension, in rate-sensitive equities — will structurally increase.

The irony is not lost on anyone. Trump nominated Warsh expecting rate cuts. Warsh promised independence from the White House. And the first data point on his watch is screaming rate hike.

## Leveraged ETF Exposure Is the Specific Risk

The direct threat is to high-beta, rate-sensitive long positions — most acutely, leveraged ETFs.

In a rising rate environment, leveraged ETFs face a two-sided squeeze:

1. **Valuation compression**: Rising rates lower the present value of future earnings. TQQQ (3x Nasdaq 100) and SOXL (3x Semiconductor Index) are concentrated in high-multiple growth stocks — the most rate-sensitive equities in the market.

2. **Carry cost expansion**: Leveraged ETFs use swaps and borrowed capital to achieve 3x exposure. When short-term rates rise, the daily reset mechanism becomes more costly, widening tracking error against the underlying index.

The 2022 precedent is instructive: when the Fed raised rates 425bps over 12 months, the Nasdaq 100 fell ~33%. TQQQ fell **79.6%** over the same period — not 3x the index loss, but nearly 2.4x due to volatility decay on the daily rebalancing.

A 39% probability of a rate hike is not a certainty. But it is a material risk that was priced at near-zero six months ago.

## Reduce Leverage Exposure — Watch Duration

**Position: Cautious / Reducing leveraged beta**

The data supports the following framework for the next 60-90 days:

The base case remains rates on hold — Warsh is hawkish but will likely want several months of data before acting. Nvidia-driven Nasdaq strength provides some cushion. However, the distribution of outcomes has shifted: the tail risk of a rate hike is now real and growing.

**Actionable adjustments I am making:**

1. **Trimming TQQQ/SOXL to no more than 10% of risk capital.** The asymmetry is unfavorable: the upside from holding is capped by valuations already stretched after SOX's +64% run since end of March; the downside from a rate hike shock is outsized.

2. **Rotating into QQQ (unleveraged) and short-duration Treasuries.** Same exposure to Nasdaq tech leadership without the leverage cost drag in a rising-rate environment.

3. **Monitoring June PPI and CPI as the next decision trigger.** If June data confirms the April trajectory, the 39% probability becomes a 55%+ probability, and positioning must shift more aggressively defensive.

4. **Avoiding energy sector shorts.** The geopolitical driver (Iran) is not resolved. Energy stocks hedge the inflation risk in the portfolio.

One more thing worth noting: the PHLX Semiconductor Index has risen **+64% since end of March**. That is a significant run in 6 weeks. Whether that advance is sustainable in a rising-rate environment — or whether it represents a distribution opportunity — will depend almost entirely on what the Fed does next.

## Bottom Line

Warsh inherits a central bank under pressure. PPI at +1.4% MoM and CPI at 3.8% YoY have closed the window for rate cuts and opened the door for rate hikes. The narrowness of his confirmation (54-45) signals political division that could complicate future policy credibility.

For the portfolio: reduce leveraged beta, extend duration hedge, and stay close to the data. The next 60 days of inflation prints will define the trajectory.
