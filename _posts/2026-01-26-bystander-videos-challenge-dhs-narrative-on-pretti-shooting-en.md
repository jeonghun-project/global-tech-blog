## The Scoop: Narrative vs. Reality 🔨

A significant divergence has emerged between official statements and on-the-ground evidence regarding the shooting of Alex Pretti in Minneapolis.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) officially stated that Pretti, a 37-year-old ICU nurse, "violently resisted" and brandished a 9mm handgun. However, **CNBC-verified bystander videos directly contradict this account**.

This isn't just a local crime story; it's a structural stress test for the new administration's aggressive enforcement policies.

## The Evidence: Frame by Frame 🎞️

The video evidence verified by CNBC paints a disturbing picture of the engagement:

*   **Video 1**: Pretti is seen **retreating** from agents, holding what appears to be a phone, not a weapon.
*   **Video 2**: Pretti intervenes only after an agent shoves a woman. He is met with chemical spray, not verbal de-escalation.
*   **The Critical Moment**: Footage suggests that agents had already restrained Pretti and **removed a weapon from his possession** before the fatal shots were fired.

## Why It Matters: Systemic Risk 📉

From a risk perspective, this incident highlights a breakdown in operational discipline and public communication.

1.  **Credibility Gap**: The rapid release of a narrative ("he brandished a gun") that is easily debunked by bystander video erodes trust in federal reporting.
2.  **Policy Blowback**: This incident could catalyze significant civil unrest, complicating the administration's domestic agenda.
3.  **Legal Liability**: If agents fired *after* disarming the suspect, the legal and financial ramifications for the agency will be severe.
