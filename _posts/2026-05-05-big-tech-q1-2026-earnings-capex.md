## What Actually Changed This Quarter

Three structural shifts are worth tracking:

**1. Cloud re-acceleration is not a blip.**
Google Cloud at $20B (+63%) and AWS beating estimates by $1.10/share are not one-quarter events. Enterprise AI adoption — Gemini-powered enterprise tools, Amazon Bedrock, Azure OpenAI — is converting into signed contracts with multi-year commitments. The lag between AI investment and cloud revenue recognition is closing.

**2. Meta's AI ad engine is compounding.**
Meta's EPS of $7.31 vs. $6.79 expected reflects something deeper than revenue growth. Its AI-powered ad recommendation system is improving both CPM (cost per thousand impressions) and CTR (click-through rate) simultaneously. When both metrics rise together, revenue scales non-linearly. This is margin expansion in real time.

**3. Microsoft's Copilot flywheel is beginning to register.**
GitHub Copilot crossed significant enterprise subscription milestones. Azure AI workloads showed no deceleration. At $4.27 EPS, Microsoft is demonstrating that enterprise software + AI can maintain premium pricing power even in a rate-hold environment.

## The $700 Billion Problem Nobody Wants to Talk About

Here is where the story gets complicated.

These four companies are now guiding to combined 2026 CapEx in excess of **$700 billion** — a number that keeps increasing each quarter. Amazon, despite the massive EPS beat, is seeing free cash flow (FCF) compressed precisely because of this CapEx surge.

The bull/bear debate on this is clean:

**Bull case**: Today's $700B in AI infrastructure CapEx is tomorrow's moat. If AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud become the default compute fabric for global AI workloads, this expenditure yields the highest-ROI capital deployment in corporate history. The competitive barriers constructed today will be extremely difficult to replicate in 2028-2030.

**Bear case**: FCF compression limits shareholder return capacity (buybacks, dividends). In a rate-hold environment — Fed stuck at 3.50%-3.75% with no cuts in sight — high-multiple stocks require FCF growth to justify valuations. These companies are running counter to that requirement right now.

The market has voted. It is taking the bull side. But this is a bet on execution over a 3-5 year horizon, not a near-term trade.

## Competitive Context

The CapEx arms race has implications beyond these four companies:

- **NVIDIA** remains the primary beneficiary of the compute buildout. $700B in infrastructure CapEx flows heavily through GPU procurement.
- **Custom silicon** is the counter-narrative. Amazon Trainium, Google TPUs, and Microsoft's Maia chips are scaling. The long-run risk for NVIDIA is internal chip development reducing external GPU dependency — but this is 2028+ story.
- **Hyperscaler consolidation**: The gap between AWS/Azure/Google Cloud and tier-2 cloud providers is widening. Oracle Cloud and Snowflake are scaling, but the infrastructure moat is being built by players spending $700B — not $7B.

## My Verdict: Bullish on AI Infrastructure, Cautious on Near-Term Multiples

**Positioning:**

The AI monetization thesis has now been empirically confirmed. Q1 2026 is the data point that validates the investment cycle began in 2023. The question is no longer "will AI generate revenue?" — it is "at what valuation does AI revenue growth justify current multiples?"

My current stance:

- **Overweight AMZN**: The +65% EPS surprise reflects AWS re-acceleration that the street was underestimating. The CapEx/FCF headwind is real, but the revenue trajectory is now on a steeper path.
- **Hold MSFT**: Azure + Copilot flywheel is intact. Premium multiple is warranted but limits near-term upside relative to AMZN.
- **Hold GOOGL**: Cloud at $20B is the real story. A one-penny EPS miss on $110B revenue is not a concern. Watch for further cloud margin expansion.
- **Trim META on strength**: The AI ad engine is operating excellently. However, at current multiples and with CapEx guidance increasing again, risk/reward favors a modest trim for tactical purposes.

**Key risks to monitor:**
1. Fed rate-hold into late 2026 → high-multiple compression risk
2. FCF trajectory: if CapEx continues rising faster than revenue, FCF yield deteriorates
3. Custom silicon adoption: any acceleration reduces GPU moat and NVIDIA's pricing power
4. Powell succession (May 15): Kevin Warsh as incoming Fed chair introduces policy uncertainty
